# Developer-documentation checklist

## Voice

Use accessible technical language. Address the reader without treating a task
as easy or trivial. Cut slang, promotional claims, and unnecessary formality.

Source: Google's [voice and tone](https://developers.google.com/style/tone).

## Procedures

Introduce a procedure with its context and goal. Put prerequisites before the
actions that need them. Use ordered steps when sequence matters and begin each
step with an imperative action. State the tool or location before the action
where needed. Label optional steps. Include a result when it helps the reader
continue or check completion. Keep one main action per step where practical.

Source: Google's [procedures](https://developers.google.com/style/procedures).

## Tense

Use present tense for general or current product behavior. Keep future tense
when it describes a real event that will happen later, including asynchronous
behavior. Do not use future tense merely to describe how a feature will work
after a product release.

Source: Google's [present tense](https://developers.google.com/style/tense).

## Project review pass

Before returning the revision:

- Check that a shorter instruction still contains its conditions.
- Preserve the spelling and case of technical tokens.
- Distinguish a documented expected result from a result actually observed.
- Keep an unresolved technical question visible.

These review steps are project editorial practice. They do not add rules to
the upstream guide. See [the notice](../NOTICE.md) for attribution and changes.
